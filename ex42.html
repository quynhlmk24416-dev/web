Run the Messier Marathon

It's time for the annual Messier Marathon. Instead of racing up the stairs, amateur astronomers will be hunting for telescopes, star charts, and a hefty cup of coffee in an all-night vigil hunting the Messier objects.

Messier objects are stellar objects, classified by astronomer Charles Messier in the 18th century, ranging from distant galaxies to star clusters to stellar nebulae.

March is the only month of the year in which all 110 Messier objects are in the sky. Unfortunately, if you want to see all of them, you have to start looking right after sunset and continue until just before sunrise.

Ironically, Charles Messier wasn't all that interested in these objects. He made the list in order to avoid seeing them. Messier was more interested in discovering new comets.
